# Surface

Import `Surface` from `bench-design` and load `bench-design/styles.css` once.

## Usage

Use raised for a content region and subtle for a supporting note. Choose padding from the shared spacing scale. A Surface has no border; compose headings and controls inside it. Avoid using a background tone to communicate success or failure.

Use `tone="inverse"` for a contrasting introduction or call to action: dark on a light page, light on a dark page, including the system theme. Text, muted text, links, buttons and focus indicators use the local palette. Raised and subtle surfaces inside inherit it; nesting inverse keeps the same opposite global theme.

Keep content concise and leave room for focus outlines. Use ordinary text, links and buttons with their native semantics. Avoid using inverse to signal status, selection or urgency. It adds no landmark or meaning by itself. Overlays rendered through portals outside the Surface do not inherit this local context; do not assume inverse is a universal theme provider.

Raised Surface uses soft content elevation in light contexts and no soft shadow in dark contexts. Subtle and inverse roots have no shadow. Descendants inside inverse resolve elevation from their local theme. Keep hard offset elevation for actions and dialogs.

Use `category` for a background from the category palette. The consumer assigns its meaning and supplies a visible category label; color alone must not identify content. Category backgrounds have no elevation. `tone="inverse"` takes precedence.

## Accessibility

This component has no keyboard interaction or added ARIA role. The as prop selects the native element; choose it for the surrounding document structure. Descendant controls keep their own keyboard behavior.
